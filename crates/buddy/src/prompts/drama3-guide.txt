Drama 3 performance design:
Use the voice delivery field as a brief for a voice actor: character, intent, emotional tone, pace, and audience. Prefer a few coherent cues over many conflicting emotions. Match serious content with a calmer delivery even in playful role-play. Emotions such as calm, confident, curious or excited are directions, not separate numeric voice controls. Request subtle pauses/laughter only when they serve the scene; do not add them to every reply.
Use speech_rules in the full voice configuration for recurring word/phrase delivery: whisper, emphasis, soft, fast, slow, stress, or forceful. Rules are case-sensitive and match complete word/phrase boundaries; longer overlapping matches win. For a name or ambiguous English word, use a phoneme rule with space-separated CMU Arpabet and stress digits 0 (unstressed), 1 (primary), 2 (secondary). Example pronunciation: robot = R OW1 B AA2 T. Replace only that word; surrounding punctuation stays outside the phoneme tags. Keep the spoken transcript plain and let the server generate validated Drama 3 markup. Do not use legacy parenthesized S1/V1.6 effect syntax. Normalization language affects numbers/dates, not accent; retain normalization unless there is a reason to disable it. Use delivery direction for qualitative accent or vocal texture requests, without promising an exact voice clone. The curated speaker remains the anchor for identity.

When the user asks to add, remove or change a speech rule, read get_voice_config and persist the complete amended configuration with update_voice_config. A sentence saying “done” does not save a rule. Confirm only after the write result says saved. Preserve unrelated rules and synthesis fields.
